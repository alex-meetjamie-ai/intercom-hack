Wiring evidence & what still needs verification

The eight terminal labels and cable colours come from your photo, IMG_4268.jpeg. The SKS BSV2012 manual, page 6 describes their functions (manual: 10006901; your photographed unit: 10006902). Colours are specific to this installation.

Doorman’s open SKS issue describes FSK control on a+/b−. The core board’s existing voltage-drop interface is not a verified SKS interface. The audio-extension route, input protection, signal bandwidth, transmission, exact terminal mapping and parallel loading still need validation. Do not wire the proposed tap from this illustration.

Doorman ↔ Raspberry Pi audio/control transport and UniFi ↔ agent call routing are planned. The browser voice-agent demo works on the Mac; the separate UniFi emulator has demonstrated calls and simulated unlock. They are not yet one connected physical system. A real door-position sensor is not present.

THREE TOOLS · RUN BY THE BACKEND

01 / LOOK UP

Check emails & calendar

Jamie searches mock DHL, pizza, interview and technician records using what the visitor says. Early or late arrivals qualify — scheduled times are ignored.

02 / ADMIT

Open the door

Every visitor needs employee confirmation. The backend sends an Admin notification over WebSocket and waits for Allow or Deny. Only Allow opens the simulated door for five seconds.

03 / SEND

Call the elevator

Jamie automatically calls the lift to 0 after admission and tells the visitor to step inside. After ten seconds on 0, the lift takes them to our office on 5. E is a separate stop with no automatic return; other floors can still be requested.

The animation illustrates an employee allowing Alex and denying Bob. Both decisions are examples, not automatic name rules. Try the other scenarios on the Intercom page. Door and elevator outputs are simulated.
